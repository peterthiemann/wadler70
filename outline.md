# All You Need Is `refl` --- Almost

## The question

* Substitution: a solved problem?
  * formalizing programming-language metatheory
  * formalizing binders and substitution
  * manual reasoning is often haphazard and error prone
  * mechanized reasoning still offers a plethora of representations and proof styles
* What would it mean to make substitution bureaucracy disappear?
  * not merely shorter proofs
  * the desired equations should hold by computation

## The mechanization landscape (briefly)

* Traditional encodings require substantial infrastructure for renaming and substitution.
* Automation such as Autosubst demonstrates that much of this work can be generated.
* Our target is different:
  * native computation inside Agda
  * intrinsically typed, inductive term syntax
  * useful substitution equations proved by `refl`

## Same surface syntax, different representation of variables

* The examples use a common intrinsically typed λ-calculus:

      A, B ::= `ℕ | A ⇒ B
      Γ, Δ ::= ∅ | Γ ▷ A
      Γ ⊢ A                         terms of type A in context Γ

* Use the same presentation syntax for terms in EW and RoR:

      ●   : Γ ▷ A ⊢ A
      _↑  : Γ ⊢ A → Γ ▷ B ⊢ A
      ƛ_  : Γ ▷ A ⊢ B → Γ ⊢ A ⇒ B
      _·_ : Γ ⊢ A ⇒ B → Γ ⊢ A → Γ ⊢ B

* In Explicit Weakening, `●`, `_↑`, `ƛ_`, and `_·_` are term constructors.
  * substitutions are intrinsically typed data generated by `id`, weakening `σ ↑`, and cons `σ ▷ M`
  * `σ : Γ ⊨ Δ` maps variables of `Δ` to terms in `Γ`
* In Return of the Renamings, the aligned notation expands to:

      ●       ≔ var here
      ƛ N     ≔ lam N
      L · M   ≔ app L M
      M ↑     ≔ M [ wkᴿ ]ᴿ
      M [ σ ] ≔ M [ σ ]ˢ

  * standard de Bruijn variables are generated by `here` and `there`
  * `var x` injects a variable `x : Γ ∋ A` into a term
  * `ρ : Ren Δ Γ` maps variables to variables
  * `σ : Sub Δ Γ` maps variables to terms
* The direction of substitution is the same despite the different notation:

      M : Δ ⊢ A      EW: σ : Γ ⊨ Δ      RoR: σ : Sub Δ Γ
      -----------------------------------------------------
                            M [ σ ] : Γ ⊢ A

* Main takeaway: the surface notation is shared, but its computational status differs.
  * in EW, `●` and `_↑` are constructors
  * in RoR, `●` is an abbreviation and `_↑` is derived renaming
* The aliases used in the presentation live in `STLC70Notation.agda`.
* Keep the complete grammars, raw constructor signatures, lifting definitions, and composition directions on a backup slide.

## What does `refl` mean?

* Distinguish three ways to establish an equality in Agda:
  * an explicit propositional proof
  * definitional equality by ordinary normalization
  * definitional equality extended by user-defined rewrite rules
* Running example:

      N ↑ [ M ]₀ ≡ N

* The goal: arrange the representation and rewrite theory so that useful substitution equations normalize to the same term.

## The equational gold standard: the σ-calculus

* Abadi, Cardelli, Curien, and Lévy, *Explicit Substitutions*, JFP 1991.
* A unified calculus of terms and explicit substitutions.
  * untyped and intrinsically typed presentations
  * substitution application and composition governed by rewrite rules
  * confluent and terminating
* The full benchmark, including substitution η, is solved by `refl`.
  * introduction
  * double substitution
  * commuting substitution
  * substitution through η-expansion and single substitution
  * η-id and η-law
* Why this is a gold standard rather than our final Agda representation:
  * in the direct Agda realization, terms and substitutions are governed by the rewrite theory rather than available as ordinary inductive data
  * the metatheory and its rewrite system are tied to the particular calculus
* Design question:

      How much of the σ-calculus can we retain while recovering inductive syntax?

## Explicit Weakening: regain inductive syntax

* Wadler's representation:
  * terms are intrinsically typed inductive data
  * substitutions are intrinsically typed inductive data
  * a variable is represented by the blob `●` plus explicit weakening `_↑`
* Instantiation is a function `Term → Sub → Term`.
  * case analysis on the substitution, then on the term
* Composition is a function `Sub → Sub → Sub`.
  * case analysis on the second substitution, then on the first
* Three proved equations are installed as rewrite rules:
  * composition and instantiation: `M [ σ ] [ τ ] ≡ M [ σ ⨾ τ ]`
  * left identity
  * associativity
* The payoff:
  * introduction, double substitution, and commuting substitution are `refl`
  * the syntax remains inductive and intrinsically typed

## Explicit Weakening is weaker than the gold standard

* Explicit weakening changes which terms are syntactically distinguishable.
* A decisive witness: weakening does not commute with η-expansion.

      ((ƛ ((N ↑) · ●)) [ id ↑ ])
        ≢
      ƛ (((N [ id ↑ ]) ↑) · ●)

  * the two sides normalize to different outer constructors
  * no propositional proof exists for the uniform equation
* Further failures include substitution through η-expansion, substitution through single substitution, η-id, and η-law.
* Show one concrete critical pair witnessing why the desired additional rules cannot simply be added while retaining the required confluence/subject-reduction properties in Agda.
* This motivates restoring ordinary variables and renamings.

## Return of the Renamings

* Terms remain intrinsically typed inductive data.
* Variables are standard de Bruijn variables.
* Renamings are first class again.
* The substitution representation is hidden behind an equational interface.
  * the current model uses functions from variables to terms
  * instantiation is structural on terms
  * composition and interaction are governed by a selected set of σ-calculus equations
* The selected rewrite theory is compatible with the combined reduction and rewrite system.
  * state precisely what confluence result is used or proved
  * display the small set of equations that matters for the examples

## RoR is strictly stronger than Explicit Weakening

* Every equality validated by Explicit Weakening is preserved by the translation into RoR.
* Strictness witness: weakening commutes with η-expansion in RoR.

      (ƛ ((N ↑) · ●)) ↑
        ≡
      ƛ (((N ↑) ↑) · ●)

  * the equation is `refl` in RoR
  * here `_↑` expands to renaming by `wkᴿ`
  * its Explicit Weakening analogue is impossible
* The more general substitution-through-η and substitution-through-single-substitution examples are also `refl`.

## RoR is still weaker than the σ-calculus

* RoR deliberately omits substitution η from its rewrite theory:
  * η-id

        (var₀ ∙ˢ wkˢ) ≡ idˢ

  * η-law

        (var₀ [ σ ]ˢ) ∙ˢ (wkˢ ⨟ˢ σ) ≡ σ

* These equations belong to the full σ-calculus but not to RoR's definitional equality.
* A concrete function-valued model may establish corresponding extensional equalities propositionally; they are not rewrite rules and do not make the general term equation `refl`.
* This is the precise meaning of "almost."

## The design space

* Compare the three systems on the two axes that matter:

  | system | inductive term syntax | variables | equational strength |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | σ-calculus | no, in the direct Agda realization | unified term/substitution calculus | gold standard; includes η-id and η-law |
  | Return of the Renamings | yes | standard de Bruijn variables and first-class renamings | strictly weaker; omits substitution η |
  | Explicit Weakening | yes | `●` plus explicit weakening | strictly weaker again |

* Summary hierarchy, under the intended translations and benchmark equations:

      σ-calculus  ⊋  Return of the Renamings  ⊋  Explicit Weakening

## Conclusion

* The σ-calculus is the equational gold standard: the complete benchmark is computation.
* Explicit Weakening recovers inductive syntax, but its blob-and-weakening representation gives up useful equalities.
* Return of the Renamings keeps inductive syntax and standard de Bruijn variables while recovering a strictly stronger equational theory.
* The remaining gap is deliberate and exact: substitution η is outside RoR's rewrite theory.
* All you need is `refl` --- almost.

## Backup: benchmark equations

* Introduction:

      N ↑ [ M ]₀ ≡ N

* Double substitution:

      N [ M ]₁ [ L ]₀ ≡ N [ L ↑ ]₀ [ M ]₀

* Commuting substitution:

      N [ M ]₀ [ L ]₀ ≡ N [ L ]₁ [ M [ L ]₀ ]₀

* Substitution through η-expansion:

      (ƛ (N ↑ · ●)) [ σ ] ≡ ƛ ((N [ σ ]) ↑ · ●)

* Substitution through single substitution:

      (N [ id ▷ M ]) [ σ ]
        ≡
      (N [ σ ↑ ▷ ● ]) [ id ▷ (M [ σ ]) ]

* Weakening through η-expansion:

      ((ƛ ((N ↑) · ●)) [ id ↑ ])
        ≡
      ƛ (((N [ id ↑ ]) ↑) · ●)

* η-id:

      ((id ↑) ▷ ●) ≡ id

* η-law:

      (((id ↑) ⨾ σ) ▷ (● [ σ ])) ≡ σ
